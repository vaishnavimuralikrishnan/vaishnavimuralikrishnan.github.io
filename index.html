About

My academic background began in Physics before transitioning into Data Science and enterprise AI engineering. Working with ontology-driven systems, semantic reasoning, and knowledge graphs led me toward a broader question: how do neural systems develop internal representations capable of supporting reasoning without explicitly encoded structure?

My current work explores representation learning through independent experiments involving Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPA), latent world models, and physics-inspired environments. Rather than treating predictive performance as the primary objective, I am interested in understanding when learned representations become editable, reusable, and capable of supporting abstraction.

Independent Research

Intervening on Latent Representations in JEPA World Models

A comparative study between autoregressive Transformers and JEPA-style latent predictors using a controlled physics simulation of elastic collisions.

The project investigates whether intervention on learned latent representations provides evidence of abstraction beyond predictive accuracy.
