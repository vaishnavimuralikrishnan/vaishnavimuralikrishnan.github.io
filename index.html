About

I trained as a physicist, then moved through data science and into industry work on ontology-driven knowledge graphs — RDF, OWL, SPARQL, the machinery of representing what a system knows explicitly. Alongside that I've kept doing observational astronomy for the sheer pleasure of measuring something real and being unsure what it means, and more recently I've been building small world models to poke at how learned systems represent the things they predict.

None of it feels like a detour to me — physics gave me a way of asking what a "state" is and how it evolves; knowledge engineering gave me a feel for the difference between a representation that's structured on purpose and one that just happens to work. I like being wrong about something in a way that teaches me why, more than being routinely right.

Independent project · 2025

World models and causal structure in latent space

schematic: intervening on the latent state vs. the pixel state mid-rollout

A comparison of a Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (64-dim latent space) against a GPT-style autoregressive pixel Transformer, both trained on a two-body elastic-collision simulation. The Transformer's near-perfect one-step loss turned out to be memorization of a deterministic trajectory, not learned dynamics — the JEPA's higher loss was a real information bottleneck, not a weaker model.

Manually perturbing the JEPA's latent vector mid-rollout produced dynamics that continued coherently from the new state — a manipulable belief state, which I'm careful to describe as a controlled observation, not a claim of general causal reasoning.

pymunk · PyTorch · latent intervention
Observational paper · 2025

Astrometric observations of WDS 18380+4151

separation measurements, 1999–2025 — historical data in green, our 2025 point in red

Ten CCD observations of a double-star system with the Great Basin Observatory, combined with nine historical measurements and Gaia DR3 parallax and proper-motion data, to weigh the evidence for a physical versus optical binary. The honest answer is: still inconclusive — which is itself the point of doing astrometry carefully.

AstroImageJ · ASTAP · Gaia DR3
Undergraduate research

CMEs and solar energetic particles, cycles 23 & 24

Analyzed 152 SEP events from SOHO/LASCO data, finding a 22% drop in mean CME speed between solar cycles, and used Type II radio burst band-splitting to estimate coronal Alfvén speed.
